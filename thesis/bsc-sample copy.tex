\section*{Abstract}

This thesis examines the problem of lane-marking segmentation from LiDAR data by framing it as a simplified point-cloud semantic segmentation task. It focuses on the design of a structured and reproducible experimental pipeline, including data preparation, representation, and baseline model configuration. A reduced label space and a standard segmentation architecture are used to establish a clear and controlled reference setting, with particular attention to challenges such as class imbalance and sparse target features. Rather than proposing a novel method, the work aims to provide a stable foundation for analyzing the capability of LiDAR-based approaches to capture lane-related structure in complex driving scenes, supporting subsequent evaluation and refinement.
